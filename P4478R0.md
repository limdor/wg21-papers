# Abstract

C++26 DIS was not approved, and WG21 needs to decide how to proceed. The goal of this paper is to explain the only option the author believe its feasible, not only short term but also long term.

In summary:

- Abandon C++26
- Keep the draft as it is
- If an alternative to the current contracts proposal become mature enough, replace the current contracts for the new alternative
- Do not add new major features into the draft
- Until a new standard is published, focus on bug fixes
- Once WG21 and ISO NB have consensus publish a new ISO C++ standard

# Introduction

On October 24th I was made aware that the C++26 DIS was not approved. The main controversy was contracts.
Since then, I saw a lot of different proposals on how to proceed, but most of them seemed wrong to me.

I believe that compromise is the only way to move forward, but most of these proposals were not about compromise. Compromise means that in the presence of different parties, all parties give up something to settle their differences. In most proposals that I have seen, I was unable to see how the different parties were giving up something. In most of them, only some of the parties were giving up something.

This papers makes a proposal that when applied in its entirety, I believe it is real compromise. I am aware that people might not like it (writing this paper does not mean I like it), but compromise is sometimes exactly that, everyone goes back home unhappy.

# The different parties

To understand if there is compromise, first we need to understand who are the different parties. This is a simplification, but in a standard where hundreds of experts are involved, that is the only feasible way to proceed. In my simplification there are 3 parties: the ones in favor of the current contracts, the ones against the current contracts, and the others. It is important to notice that I mention also the others. I believe this is a big enough group that should not be omitted.

## Pro current contracts

This group represents people who are happy enough with the current contracts proposal that they do not want it removed from the standard. This might include original authors, implementers, and people who have spend a lot of hours of their life trying to make this proposal as good as possible.

## Against current contracts

This group represents people who think that the current contracts proposal is so bad that it cannot be fixed in future standard versions and it should be removed from the standard. This group might include people that want to see contracts in the standard but not necessary following the current design.

## The others

This group is the rest of people that are not included in the other two groups. This could be potentially subdivided in two other groups: the ones that prioritize short term, and the ones that prioritize long term. For the purpose of this proposal I keep them together, but both are legit and both should be considered.
The others group might include people who are fine on keeping contracts in the standard, but they could also accept removing contracts if this means publishing a new version of the standard. It might also include people who have no opinion at all about contracts but still might care about other features or the future of C++ (short or long term). This group might also include people from the Third Team (See [@P4430R0]).

# About me

It is extremely hard to write a proposal without personal bias and that is why I believe it is important to be transparent about my position for such a topic. I work on safety critical systems, and I care about functional safety. I define myself as a person belonging to [The others](#the-others) group. At the beginning I was against contracts, believing that it contained too much implementation defined behavior. With time, I came to the conclusion that it would be very hard to come up with something better. This left me with choosing between the current contracts design or nothing at all. The topic was important enough for me that I spent enough time to feel comfortable and take an informed decision.

# The proposal in detail

## Summary and goal

The goals of this proposal are:

- Keep C++ an international standard
- Avoid forks of the language into different dialects maintained by different organizations or technical experts
- Incentivize collaboration to achieve maximum consensus
- Prevent that a majority can ignore a minority
- Prevent that a minority can overrule a majority

The idea is to force WG21 and NBs to understand that they cannot ignore each other. If NBs want to have a new feature, they need to take the full C++ draft or nothing. If WG21 wants to have a new feature, they need to convince the NBs that it is worth standardizing.

To achieve that, the steps are listed below, and detailed in the following sections. This proposal considers all or nothing. It is not thought in a way that only some of them could be chosen.

- Abandon C++26
- Keep the draft as it is
- If an alternative to the current contracts proposal become mature enough, replace the current contracts for the new alternative
- Do not add new major features into the draft
- Until a new standard is published, focus on bug fixes
- Once WG21 and ISO NB have consensus publish a new ISO C++ standard

## Abandon C++26

Doing minor changes into the draft will not satisfy NBs. This leave us only with the option of doing major changes.

Considering that the controversial feature is contracts, we have two options when it comes to major changes. Do major improvements (i.e. labels, enforced preconditions, etc) or remove contracts from the draft.

We are too late into the cycle to do major improvements. Each C++ cycle has two main phases: adding features, and fixing issues. If now we do major changes to contracts, we would have to delay C++26 to be able to fix the issues that these major improvements might introduce. This would bring C++26 very close to the C++29 timeline. This is the reason why doing major improvements for contracts is not an option.

The other options is to remove the current contracts from C++. This might satisfy NBs but it is a disrespect for WG21 and all the technical experts that worked on contracts and decided to bring it into the C++ draft with consensus. Removing contracts from the draft, it also opens the door in the future for the NBs to do the same with other features. It also poisons any future discussion in WG21.

This leave us with the only option of skipping C++26.

## Keep the draft as it is

Removing contracts from the draft without technical reasons, sends the message to the NBs that once WG21 presents a draft, they can pick the features they want. Also the draft should only be modified because of technical reasons, not for political reasons. WG21 are the experts that come up with a draft, if NBs do not like it, then WG21 needs to come back with a new proposal.

As of today, there is not enough consensus in WG21 to remove contracts from the draft due to technical reasons. If such technical reasons appear, the removal should be reconsidered. Until then, the removal should not happen because of political reasons.

## If an alternative to the current contracts proposal become mature enough, replace the current contracts for the new alternative

Right now there is no alternative to the current contracts design that would have enough consensus inside WG21. It would not be fair to remove contracts from the standard just because at some point we might have a better alternative. However, it would also not be fair to keep the current contracts design if a better alternative appears.
Because of that, the proposal is that if an alternative to the current contract design appears, WG21 should vote the replacement of the current contracts design for the new contracts design.
This gives the option to group in favor of the current contract design to keep improving it, and the option to a group with an alternative to bring it to the standard.

## Do not add new major features into the draft

With this proposal, the publication of a new ISO standard would be hold for a period arbitrary long. The reduce the time until the next new standard as much as possible, the proposal is to not accept any more major feature in the C++ draft. Adding new major features would just bring risk of increased disagreement between NBs and WG21. The key here is to decide what is a major feature or not, that would be the call of the chairs and the convenor but everyone would be invited to raise their voice if they believe a proposal might add just risk. Blocking major features would also be an incentive for NBs and WG21 wanting new features to find an agreement.

## Until a new standard is published, focus on bug fixes

There have been several voices saying that C++ needs to slow down on adding so many new major features. We still have major features from C++20 with bugs and not fully implemented. Implementers claim to support C++23 but they struggle to keep up with the pace. CWG and LWG have a none significant amount of specification issues.

If we stop adding major features until a new standard is published, we would the chance to make the current C++ specification better.

## Once WG21 and ISO NB have consensus publish a new ISO C++ standard

The halt is not indefinitely, and we should strive on having consensus between WG21 and NBs as soon as possible. The proposal is to cancel C++26, but we should strive to reach this consensus for C++29.

# Alternative proposals and why I do not like them

In the C++ reflector, multiple options have been shared. Additionally, Herb Sutter published [@P4454R0], and members of the Direction Group published [@P4476R0]. Below I comment on these options compared to this proposal.

## H. Sutter option A (Skip ISO publication of C++26)

This is the closest option from [@P4454R0] to this proposal but it does not exclude adding new features to the standard. If we keep adding new features, the risk of disagreement in C++29 increases.

It also does not consider the option that the current contracts design could be replaced by another design. This prevents NBs that are against the current design, to propose a different design that it is technically better.

## H. Sutter option B (Remove contracts and retry DIS + rehear alternatives)

I find that choosing this option would be disrespectful for WG21 and would send the message that NBs can veto any feature that they do not like in future drafts. It might be hard to recover the trust between WG21 and NBs after this.

## H. Sutter option C (Re-ballot post-Croydon draft and campaign)

NBs might end up with the feeling that are completely ignored. It might be hard to recover the trust between WG21 and NBs after this.

## H. Sutter option D (Ballot the post-Búzios draft as DIS (possibly with P3911))

It is hard to know if NBs would accept such option. This could be something worth a try, and if it does not work, it does not prevent to continue with what this paper proposes. The risk is that if it works, some of the last minute changes might introduce some unforeseen issues.

## H. Sutter option E (Ballot post-Búzios draft as national C++ standard(s))

Going from an international standard to a national standard is the first step until we end up with multiple standards for the same language. This also goes against the goal of this proposal that wants to keep C++ as an international standard.

## H. Sutter option F (Option E + investigate making all experts equal by moving into an open NB (ANSI) or LF)

This goes against the goal of this proposal that wants to keep C++ as an international standard.

## Direction Group Option 1 (Circulate a revised enquiry draft)

I feel that it focuses too much on delivering on scheduled without considering the long term consequences. The idea of the 3 years cycle is that we do not wait for a feature to publish a standard. However here we have a different situation, a feature is already in the draft but the NBs do not accept the draft. It has 3 sub options.

### Option 1.a (Revised draft almost identical)

I have the same opinion like for [H. Sutter option C](#h-sutter-option-c-re-ballot-post-croydon-draft-and-campaign).

### Option 1.b (Revised draft trying to fix contracts)

I have the same opinion like for [H. Sutter option D](#h-sutter-option-d-ballot-the-post-búzios-draft-as-dis-possibly-with-p3911).

### Option 1.c (Revised draft with contracts removed)

I have the same opinion like for [H. Sutter option B](#h-sutter-option-b-remove-contracts-and-retry-dis--rehear-alternatives).

## Direction Group Option 2 (Circulate a revised committee draft for comments)

I have the same opinion like for [Option 1.c](#option-1c-revised-draft-with-contracts-removed) because it also proposes to remove contracts from the draft. For more details see [the part of my proposal](#keep-the-draft-as-it-is) on why contracts should stay in the draft.

## Direction Group Option 3 (Publish the draft as TS)

I agree with the observation of the Direction Group. I think it just makes things more complicated and it does not bring a solution moving forward.

## Direction Group Option 4 (Cancel the project)

This is basically one of the parts of this paper proposal, but it does not elaborate further. I think this option should be seriously considered but with a full plan that is more than just "Cancel the project".

# Conclusions

I would like to see C++ survive this crisis. I would also like to see all the experts that made C++ what it is today, together in the future and keep improving the language. I would like to avoid that people are left behind or feel betrayed. That is why, I believe the only way forward is compromise. This is the only option that I could find that to me truly means compromise. I believe a lot of people will not like it, but that is what compromise is about.

I do not want to push this proposal at all costs. I  just wrote it because I believe that it had to be explored and explained properly. It has been around in different forms, but I did not have the feeling that it was properly considered. This proposal is intended to just provide more information, that at least if we discard cancelling C++26, at least we know why.

I hope that in the future we see today as the point in time when all became better as experts and as persons. The moment that C++ could have ended but it just continued stronger.
